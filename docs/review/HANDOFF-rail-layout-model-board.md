## Required rail-layout semantics
The model board must consume typed layout records, never routes or literal KiCad distances.
### Row/lane solver
- Use an odd lane count.
- Anchor the named MT/MT1 main route at the center lane.
- Lay highest-priority mains first so they remain horizontally unjogged where possible.
- MT2 may move where MT/MT1 cannot; secondary mains, branches, and industries have progressively lower placement priority.
- IRJ crossings and C→N turnout crossings preserve lane.
- C→R applies exactly one adjacent-lane change based on the turnout’s source-derived orientation.
- If a lower-priority span collides with an unrelated existing span, propagate that span outward to its anchor or add one ungapped visual jog before the turnout.
- A track ending at an R-switch merges into the C→N lane and ends.
- Collision decisions are base topology decisions, not selected-route decisions.
### Board primitives
- `RailSpan` is the source of base rail geometry.
- IRJ gaps exist at actual `RailSpan` IRJ endpoints.
- One `TurnoutLayout` yields one fixed-size turnout glyph: distinct C–N segment and one-row C–R diagonal. A crossover is a separate paired-turnout/IRJ form.
- A `SignalBase` is centered on its Signal IRJ gap and placed engineer-right of its rail; no rail-to-mast spur.
- Track-circuit labels belong to spans; operating terminal names belong to `BoardTerminal`.
### Projection contract
- Dispatcher view: full rails, circuits, IRJs, turnouts, signals, terminals, and later route/aspect overlays.
- Overview view: thin main-track/passing-siding lines, CP name, and simple turnout stubs only.
- Both views use identical rail rows and longitudinal anchors. Turnouts align vertically between views.
- Named boundary mains have fixed left/right row anchors; adjacent CP boards align same-named rails at their shared seam.
## Luchessa acceptance fixture
Before routes return to the board, assert a base geography with:
- exactly three physical rails;
- explicit physical endpoints `Industry`, `Branch`, `MT`, `MT1`, and `MT2`;
- no `2SA` terminal label: it is a circuit span, not an operating endpoint;
- one glyph each for 783, 795, and 799;
- only actual IRJ gaps;
- MT/MT1, MT2, and branch/industry lanes assigned by the priority solver;
- masts on their correct engineer-right side of their Signal IRJ;
- no route-created rail, signal, terminal, or gap.
The supplied ASCII diagram and `docs/review/markup-luchessa.png` are the visual review references.
